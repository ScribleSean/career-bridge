    What the measures mean

    AI exposure (Lightcast AI Exposure & Augmentation Index) is how much of a job’s abilities, skills and tasks overlap with what AI can do today. Across Massachusetts jobs it runs from about 31.5 to 82.8. A higher score means more overlap with AI. It does not predict job loss, and high exposure can mean AI helps with the work.

    Employer demand for AI skills is the share of Massachusetts job ads for the job that ask for at least one AI skill (Lightcast, 945 skills). Exposure is the same everywhere; demand is local.

    The four groups. High exposure means an index above 50, where Lightcast’s Medium-high and High exposure bands start. High demand means at least 6.18% of MA job ads ask for AI skills, the statewide rate in 2025. Jobs with too few Massachusetts ads get a fifth group and no rate.

    The silent gap is a stricter part of the high-exposure, low-demand group: under 1% of Massachusetts job ads for the job asked for AI skills in 2025.

    What people use AI for comes from the Anthropic Economic Index: the job’s O*NET tasks that appear most in Claude.ai conversations in April–May 2026 (US). It covers one AI tool and is a share of conversations, not of workers.

    Your strengths are the O*NET skills rated more important in this job than in the average Massachusetts job. Bridge skills are the skills a next move rates most above your current job.

    Next moves are jobs with the most similar O*NET skill profile (cosine similarity, 0.70 or more) that pay at least as much in Massachusetts, need at most one more Job Zone of preparation, employ 1,000+ people in MA, and show more employer demand for AI skills than your current job.

    Limits

    • Job ads are employer demand, not hires. Small jobs in Massachusetts have too few ads for a rate.
    • Wages are OEWS medians and exclude benefits and self-employment.
    • Moves are starting points for a conversation with a career advisor, not advice for any one person.
    • No race, gender or age data is included yet.
